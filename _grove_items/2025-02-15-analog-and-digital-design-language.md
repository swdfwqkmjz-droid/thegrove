## Observations

A significant volume of web design articles in digital space are written by marketing specialists. This correlates to the focus of digital design on stats.

One name for the analog way of speaking is “archispeak”, which views the terminology as a way to make architecture sound more elite, specialized, and inaccessible to “normal” people, rather than a reflection of anything real.

I’ve noticed some overlap in ideas from sacred geometry that may apply here. Specifically, the difference could be described as a parallel between number as *quantity* (digital design) against number as *quality* (architectural or formal design).

> When Pythagoras said, 'All is arranged according to Number', he was not thinking of numbers in the ordinary, enumerative sense. In addition to simple ***quantity,*** numbers on the ideal level are possessed of ***quality,*** so that 'twoness', 'threeness' or 'fourness', for example, are not merely composed of 2, **3,** o r 4 units, but are wholes or unities in themselves, each having related powers.
>
>—Robert Lawlor, Sacred Geometry, p. 10

Ok, so I don’t know about the whole “related powers” thing, *but* there’s something to the difference of focus on relationship or form, vs. quantity or content perhaps. It’s maybe the same thing that shows up in a stereotypical engineering-oriented design—they have precision, but not soul.
